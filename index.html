01The View

Morning arrives every ninety minutes.

HALCYON circles the Earth about once every 90 minutes, at 7.7 kilometres a second. Up here a day is not a day. It is sixteen sunrises and sixteen sunsets, and you will be awake for most of them.

Stay five nights and you will watch the sun come up eighty times. Most guests stop counting around the third.

One orbit T+ 00:38:00
NightFirst lightSunriseDay

  1. Night

    First, the dark.

    The night side of the world, close enough to read. Cities are small and gold. Storms flicker without a sound.

  2. First light

    Then a thin blue line.

    The atmosphere, seen from the side. Everything that has ever breathed lives inside it. It is thinner than you expect.

  3. Sunrise

    Then the sun.

    It takes eight seconds. On Earth, dawn is slow. At 28,000 kilometres an hour it arrives all at once: ember, gold, white.

  4. Day

    And then it happens again.

    Fifty-five minutes of daylight. Thirty-five of night. You are never more than an hour from the next sunrise.

02The Journey

Ten days to prepare. Nine minutes to leave.

Nobody is rushed into orbit. You arrive in the desert, learn the ship and meet the people who will fly with you. By launch morning, very little is new.

Days 1–10 · Tabernas, Spain

Learn the ship. Meet your crew.

Ten days at our campus in the Tabernas desert. Mornings in the centrifuge and the neutral-buoyancy pool. Afternoons with your physician, your tailor and your chef. Two nights in a full-scale suite, so the real one already feels like yours.

Day 11 · Santa Maria, Azores

Eight minutes and fifty seconds.

You ride out to the pad at dawn with your crew and two Meridian pilots. The engines light. The sky goes from blue to violet to black. Eight minutes and fifty seconds after lift-off, your hands float up from the armrests.

  • 8 min 50 sFrom the pad to orbit.
  • 3.2 gAt the peak, for less than a minute.

Days 11–16 · 400 km above the Earth

Five nights. No itinerary.

Once you are aboard, the schedule is yours. Dinner is at eight, ship’s time. The Cupola never closes. The only fixed appointment is one you will not want to miss: your first sunrise, forty minutes after you arrive.

  • 80 sunrisesSixteen a day, for five days.
  • 32 guestsAt most, in twenty-four suites.
  • 14 crewHosts, chefs, engineers and two physicians.

Day 16 · North Atlantic

Home in fifty minutes.

You undock after breakfast. Fifty minutes later the capsule is under parachutes above the Atlantic, with a recovery ship waiting below. Gravity feels heavy for a day. Then three quiet nights in the Azores before the world gets you back.

  • 50 minutesFrom orbit to the sea.
  • 3 nightsOf recovery in the Azores, at your own pace.
  • 1 welcomeYour family, waiting on the quay.

05Safety & training

We will never call it routine.

Spaceflight carries real risk. Our work is to reduce it, to measure it, and to tell you the truth about it. This is how.

  1. 01

    An exit at every second

    From the launch pad to orbit, the Meridian Kestrel crew ship can separate from its rocket at any moment and bring its cabin down under parachutes.

  2. 02

    A seat home, always

    Two return ships stay docked to HALCYON at all times, with a seat for every guest and every member of crew. Either can be under way in fifteen minutes.

  3. 03

    Two physicians aboard

    Flight surgeons live on the station. Your medical baseline, taken at Tabernas, travels with you, and our clinic in Almería is on call around the clock.

  4. 04

    Three of everything that matters

    Air, pressure, power and cooling each run on three independent systems, with ninety days of reserves on board.

  5. 05

    Shielded, and watched

    Every pressurised module sits behind multi-layer debris shielding. Ground radar tracks our path, and the station moves when it should. A shielded refuge stands ready for solar storms.

  6. 06

    An independent veto

    A safety board outside our management can stop any launch, for any reason, without asking us. That is by design.
